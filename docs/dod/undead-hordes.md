# DoD: Undead hordes — waves that march to a destination, an infestation, a horde march, up to 1000 units

**Size:** L. Several deliverables (a wave destination, two new event types, a retuned template, raised unit ceilings proved by a load test) across Logic/ (Model, Validation, Limits, Apocalypse, ApocalypseHost, Spawning, Pools, CommandArgs, EventAdmin, DataStore), Services/ (ApocalypseAction, WaveAction, SpawnTracker, HuntAction), Resources/templates.json, tools/vrclient (NyarDevTools, scenarios, a ladder report) and the release surfaces; events.json gains fields and two action types, state.json's apocalypse record gains a mode.
**Planned:** interactively; the owner asked for it after the G-Portal demo of 0.13.0 (design §9 D62, Epic A46) and set the load ladder on 2026-10-17. The full procedure applies (CLAUDE.md › Development procedure): up to three review rounds, a pre-audit and a post-audit per step, a Codex cross-inspection of every step's diff, vrclient scenarios as in-game evidence, and the owner judging the look and feel.
**Request:** the owner, 2026-10-17, after the demo: "1. The ability to set a target destination for summoning waves to go from and to. 2. A separation of the zombie apocalypse, with one version being the slower build of them spawning in graveyards and spreading out, attacking NPCs and trying to essentially take over the map. With a replication mechanism for every unit that they defeat, randomly 1 to 3 additional zombies would be spawned from a pool … 3. Set up a zombie horde-specific event that is a single large Horde of undead that essentially act like a wave, like a singular large wave that goes from one location to another … for every unit they defeat along the way, they spawn additional units. If they reach the destination, then at that point they'll start to hunt down players and fight NPCs." On the scale: "up to a thousand units"; on the load test: "start with 100 and then increment up from there … 250, 500, 750, and 1,000. Ensure that the majority of those units are lower level, There should be stronger units that are essentially mixed in with the batch."

**North Star:** a server admin can send any wave from a start to a destination, run an undead infestation that rises at the graveyards and grows with every kill, and run a horde march of up to 500 mostly weak undead with stronger ones and a boss that grows on the way and infests the map at its destination, with up to 1000 tracked units once the load ladder proves the server takes them, all off by default and inside the caps, released as 0.14.0; finished when D1-D32 have evidence.

## Purpose & typical use
The owner's players gather at a town for Halloween night. The admin stands in the square and starts `undead-horde-march`: a horde of 200 rises at the nearest graveyard 300 m or more away, 160 skeletons and ghouls, 32 guardians and mages, 8 ghosts and the Bishop of Death, and walks on the square, hidden while nobody watches and fighting wherever players ride out to meet it. Every villager or player it kills brings one to three more. If it reaches the square it breaks up and hunts the players across the map (C4). On a quieter evening the admin runs `undead-infestation`: the dead rise at five graveyards, half of them hunt the nearest players and the rest spread outward, and the map fills unless the players clear it (C3). A bandit raid can now be sent from the bandit camp to a player's gate with `action.destination here` (C2). 1.1: admins running events for groups of players; 1.2: big undead events that reach the players and feel overwhelming; 1.3: it builds on the apocalypse engine (0.13.0), the wave pools (0.13.0) and the hidden march, and raises the safety ceilings only as far as the load ladder proves (C1).

## Use cases
### Typical
The admin types `.nyar template use undead-horde-march`, raises `MaxTrackedUnits` to 600 and `MaxUnitsPerWave` to 300 in the cfg as the README says, restarts, stands at the town and types `.nyar event start undead-horde-march`. The reply names the horde size and the start. Players ride out; the horde holds where they fight it, grows on its kills, and arrives 12 minutes later; the banner says the horde has reached the living, and the units scatter to hunt. `.nyar status` shows the horde and then the infestation count.

### Minimal stretch
- 8.1 Empty and default: at the default cfg (150 tracked, 20 a wave) a horde march starts with 20 units and says it was capped by MaxUnitsPerWave 20 (D12); an infestation with no native undead in reach is refused with its reason (D8); a destination wave with zero units is refused as any wave is today.
- 8.2 Used once: a horde march started once runs to its outcome and ends; nothing is kept but the state record while it runs (D17).

### Maximal stretch
- 9.1 Volume: 1000 tracked units at the ceiling, a 500-unit horde, spread draws on many kills in one second (D3, D18, D25, D26); 1000 units spawn at 20 a tick in 50 s and drain within the margin (D3).
- 9.2 Abuse: an admin who raises the cfg past the ceilings is clamped (D1); a player cannot start or steer an event; units never enter claimed territory beyond today's rules; a farm of kills on the horde only grows it to its max (D7, D10).
- 9.3 Repeated use: a second apocalypse-type start is refused while one runs (D13); a restart mid-run resumes it (D17); stop and start again begins a fresh run.

## Business rules
1. **What spreads** (D7): a kill by a tracked unit of the running apocalypse-type event, of a player or an NPC, adds a draw of perKillMin..perKillMax from the block's pool, inside the block's max, its per-minute limit and the free slots.
2. **Origins** (D8, D10): the hotspots of the faction's native NPCs, picked by the apocalypse's rule (100 m cells, minNatives, minSpacing); a Hotspot start is the one nearest the destination at least minDistance away.
3. **Movement** (D6, D9): a front never moves while a player is within 60 m; no unit is hopped or jumped while a player is within 100 m of it or of its landing (50 m); in view, units walk under the game's AI.
4. **Precedence** (4.4): General.Enabled false or the Spawns pillar off stops everything; `.nyar purge confirm` ends every run and drains; the caps (D1) beat every count of an event; MaxConcurrentEvents counts the family as one event each; one apocalypse-type event at a time (D13); a destination event's march ends at arrival, and its behaviour then applies.
5. **"Every" sets** (4.5): every kill reaches the death hook Patches/DeathEventPatch.cs (the only death path, as the apocalypse's spread uses); every spawn goes through SpawnTracker and the ledger (Services/SpawnTracker.cs); every end path (stop, purge, pillar off, fault, duration, won, restart) ends the run and drains its units as the apocalypse's do.
- whole: the infestation's living count is the sum of its origins' units and its spread units, and a horde's is its rise, its growth and its bosses; each total is checked against the max and the free slots (D7, D10)
- siblings: Apocalypse, Infestation, HordeMarch
